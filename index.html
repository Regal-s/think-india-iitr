§ 01

A “Nation First” attitude — and what it actually means.

Think India is a pan-India movement built around a simple belief: that the best thing a young, talented person can do with their education is turn it outward — toward the nation, toward society, toward problems that actually matter.

On the IIT Roorkee campus, that belief takes the shape of late-night discussions, packed seminar halls, flag-bearing processions down campus roads, and friendships built around ideas bigger than any one person. We call it a “Nation First” attitude — not blind allegiance, but a habit of asking harder questions and taking longer views.

Our biggest moment each year is the Think India National Convention — a gathering that pulls students from IITs, NITs, IIMs, IISc and NLUs. But it is not all conferences: every Independence Day we spill onto campus roads for the Tiranga Yatra, and across the year we run melas, lectures, debates, and service drives.

§ 02

Four convictions we keep coming back to.

i.

Nation, but not nationalism.

A “Nation First” lens is not allegiance — it is a habit of asking harder questions. Who does this technology serve? Whose voice is missing from the room?

ii.

Tradition × Innovation.

Bharat is older than its borders and younger than its possibilities. We bridge Indic thought, modern science, and the engineering imagination IIT Roorkee was built on.

iii.

Thought, then action.

We think, we design, we implement — strategies that uplift urban and rural Bharat, never letting ideas stop at the seminar room.

iv.

A campus, a country, a conversation.

We sustain India-centric dialogue within the campus and across the wider intelligentsia — debates, lectures, workshops, yatras.

§ 04

Our magazine — ऋतम्.

Vol. 01 · The first issue

ऋतम् — pronounced ritam — is the chapter's first official magazine. Not just a record of events, but a canvas of innovation and a testimony to a generation's commitment to the nation.

Between its covers: the National Convention, the Tiranga Yatra, four editions of Chhatra Sansad, Mindset Makeover, Maati-NITI, Khadi Mela, Parakram and more — alongside original essays from students on Bharat's architectural marvels, the metallurgy of the Delhi Iron Pillar, and the case for Indic thought in a world of algorithms.
